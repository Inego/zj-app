# Winning a hand

You win by completing a legal [hand shape](rules-hands) and declaring
the win. How the last tile arrives affects both procedure and some
[incidental patterns (9)](pattern-9).

## Calling (ready hand)

A hand is **calling** (ready) when it is **one tile** away from a legal
win — any of several tile types may complete it (a multi-sided wait).

Example: [Nine Gates (2.2)](pattern-2.2) is a famous nine-way wait on
one [suit](rules-suits).

## Self-draw

You draw the completing tile from the wall yourself. No discarder.
Payoff: each of the other three pays the full hand value — see
[Scoring basics](scoring-basics).

## Win on discard

You claim another player's discard as the completing tile. That player
is the **discarder** (often the “responsible” payer on large hands).
The set completed by that tile is [exposed](rules-concealed-open).

## Robbing a kong

When someone declares a **small exposed kong**, if that added tile
completes your win, you may claim it for the win
([Robbing a Kong (9.3)](pattern-9.3)). Treated as a **win on discard**
for payoff. You cannot rob a **concealed** kong (including with
Thirteen Terminals).

## Interception

If more than one player claims a win on the same discard, **one** winner
is chosen by seat order from the discarder: **lower seat** (next in
turn) beats opposite, who beats upper seat. Standard Zung Jung does not
award multi-win on one discard.
